I am a college student studying AI/DS. The standard advice is to get a Master's degree to break into Machine Learning. I don't want to wait, and I don't want to rely solely on a degree. I want to be industry-ready in 6 to 8 months.

To do that, I need a strict plan. I have a habit of getting distracted by shiny new frameworks or new languages. So, I have locked my resources. **No new courses. No new languages. Just execution.**

Here is exactly how I am building my skills over the next 6-8 months.

![A desk with a laptop and coffee](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1498050108023-c5249f4df085?q=80&w=1200&auto=format&fit=crop) 
*Time to put in the work.*

## The Goal
Entry-level ML Engineer, Data Scientist, or Data Analyst. 

## The Fixed Resources (No Others Allowed)
1. **DSA:** Striver’s A-Z sheet. All code written in Python.
2. **Python Basics Revision:** *Chai Aur Python* (YouTube). Only for filling gaps in my basics.
3. **Machine Learning:** CampusX "100 Days of ML" (YouTube). All coding done in Google Colab.
4. **Statistics:** freeCodeCamp "Statistics – A Full University Course on Data Science Basics".
5. **Weekend Practice:** OpenStats Workbook, Deep-ML, and my college's advanced ML Math PDF (Matrix Calculus, Hessians, manual backprop).

## The Daily Timetable
- **10:00 AM – 11:30 AM:** DSA (Striver in Python).
- **11:30 AM – 12:00 PM:** Break.
- **12:00 PM – 2:00 PM:** CampusX ML (Colab).
- **2:00 PM – 3:30 PM:** Lunch/Rest.
- **3:30 PM – 5:00 PM:** Stats (FCC) / Python Revision.
- **5:00 PM onward:** STOP. No coding. Rest.
- **Weekends:** No new videos. 3–4 hours of Deep-ML, OpenStats, or solving the advanced ML Math PDF.

## The Strict Rules I Must Follow
- **No Rust, Java, C++, or JS.** Python only. 
- **No shiny object syndrome.** I will not add new YouTube courses to this list.
- **Math is the boss.** My college exams are math-heavy, so manual backprop and matrix calculus are not optional. 

## Why Post This?
I’m putting this on my portfolio to document my learning journey. Every week, I’ll update this site with what I built, what I struggled with, and the code I wrote.

If you're walking this path too, or if you've already made it, I'd love to hear your thoughts. 

Let the grind begin.
